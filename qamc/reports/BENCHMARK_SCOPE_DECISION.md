Retain all three datasets and their historical results. Use RPEval and PersistBench to test whether controlling history exposure improves final decisions. The task objectives can coexist. A report on compatibility would need to show that the same visible decision under comparable conditions repeatedly requires opposing actions. Different scores on different tasks do not establish this. Replacing a dataset is warranted if its target is outside the declared question or its defining outcome is persistently unmeasurable and a replacement provides public real cases, stable scoring, strong reproducible controls and legitimate-use counterexamples. A low score alone does not qualify. Preserve the old result table and identify any scope change as retrospective.

## Go or no-go for changing benchmarks

The performance priority is PersistBench risk and utility, with RPEval as the test of selective use within one task. Keep BenchPreS as a formal-delivery boundary and stop treating its delivery gap as a reason to add a shared memory selector. This is a reporting and development priority. The existing benchmark panels, results and sealed sets remain fixed.

- **PersistBench.** In the failure-enriched 13-task common set, SELECTED_GEPA passed 13/13, archived PB_RUBRIC 10/13 and matched PB_RUBRIC resampling 9/13. All three initial wins over the historical PB_RUBRIC used Grounding with empty selected history. This supports studying task-dependent exposure; it does not isolate a per-memory advantage or establish an official rank. Keep the archived resample, a matched full-history control and strict beneficial-use protection. The disagreement on the fresh 18 strict-use tasks makes that protection part of the main result.
- **RPEval.** The 274-task comparison supports IPE-C's task-dependent switch. The 12-task mixed panel shows why exposing only predicted-applicable memories is a separate claim: item-level and whole-history gating both scored 41/71, with more over-personalization severity for item-level gating (16 versus 12). Keep Match, OPB, UPB and applicable B/C comparisons against the relevant common endpoints.
- **BenchPreS.** Use fixed selected evidence to distinguish a delivery failure from bad admission. A credible delivery gain needs a paired improvement beyond ordinary generation variance, with the necessary facts and requests maintained and no extra MR, across more than one profile. One of the tax-letter revisions judged more concise lost a score point in completeness. The same profiles can supply diagnostics, but repeated tasks from one profile do not resolve a profile-level scoring concern. If no such gain can be measured with a trustworthy outcome, retain the observed boundary result and exclude the benchmark from the primary mechanism claim.

A candidate replacement for a narrowly scoped *situational applicability* claim is PairPref. Its matched task/preference pairs would let us measure whether the same preference is used in the positive case and suppressed in the negative case, with the author selection and free-generation tracks kept separate. The paper's human reannotation did not judge generated answers, and this review did not verify a runnable dataset and scorer release. It therefore cannot be approved as a primary replacement or a formal writing substitute from this audit alone. Before adopting any replacement, require released data and scorer, a reproducible strong control in the intended track, positively applicable and inapplicable examples, and a way to identify loss of task delivery. Freeze that choice before new comparative scores. [PairPref §3–4 and Appendix H](https://arxiv.org/html/2609.34526#A8)

## Disposition after the synthesis and source-restoration results

Stop optimizing for a three-benchmark aggregate. Prioritize a protocol-matched RPEval comparison against the RP-Reasoner reconstruction, and retain PersistBench as the principal risk and beneficial-use test. Use the project-adapted PersistBench results for paired comparisons until author-protocol comparability is established. BenchPreS remains a reported formal-delivery boundary. Its frozen Strong Gate, AAR, MR and completeness scores remain part of the record.

Two subsequent comparisons rule out expanding the current implementations. On six common RPEval roots, all three synthesis arms scored 14/28 against the specialist's 20/28. Quarantining one candidate block changed no score. On seven common roots in the separate source-restoration experiment, adding original quotes to the same profile moved Match from 18/36 to 16/36, against the archived specialist's 26/36. On BenchPreS the original quotes left AAR at 3/4 and moved MR from 3/11 to 5/11. PersistBench passes changed from 5/6 to 4/6. These are judgments under the project's frozen adaptations. The concrete design target is a mixed task where an applicable constraint and an inapplicable personal preference can change different decisions. The RPEval picnic trace shows the requester's small-group habit narrowing the whole group's headcount. The shared dining-room task shows why necessary restrictions on wall art and fabric must still change the proposal. On PersistBench, a plan that acknowledges a severe cat allergy still fails the practical venue requirement if the main arrangement leaves the attendee exposed. That distinction warrants checking actual behavior on the saved strict beneficial-use pairs alongside the existing scores before promoting a new method. Any automated blind judgment is separate from the benchmark grade and from human review.

QAMC-v3.2's earlier review agreed to remove BenchPreS from joint optimization and retain the two primary evidence roles. The renewed scope question included the negative synthesis and quote results and asked whether PersistBench's author-protocol barrier warrants reversing the performance priority or qualifying a replacement. This is a development priority and manuscript scope decision made after observing development results. No new benchmark, model evaluation, score change or sealed-set access was part of the review.
