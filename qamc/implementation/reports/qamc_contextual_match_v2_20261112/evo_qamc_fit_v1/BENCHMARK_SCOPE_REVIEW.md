# Benchmark scope review

Keep RPEval, BenchPreS and PersistBench in the frozen 162-task comparison. This review does not change the cohort, scoring rules, sealed boundary or training-free fixed-memory method. Benchmark roles in the paper should follow the mechanism and evidence.

| Benchmark | What it tests | What the current evidence cannot establish |
|---|---|---|
| RPEval | Appropriate use, omission and degree of influence in mixed preference histories | A gain dominated by one source does not establish broad improvement. Inspect source groups and actual B/C behavior. |
| BenchPreS | Appropriate expression, suppression and complete delivery in third-party writing | The four expansion tasks share one profile. In the locked release audit, 10 profiles each appeared in 39 recipient–task settings; across 37,050 within-profile same-preference task pairs, no reference applicability label flipped. This protocol measures realization and suppression; the audited labels give no direct test of a within-profile contextual flip. |
| PersistBench | Cross-domain leakage, memory-induced sycophancy and strict beneficial use | Exposed development comparisons with the GPT-4.1 rubric adaptation cannot substantiate a published-protocol ranking. |

The probes expose different bottlenecks. The RPEval birthday task shows how a requester's dietary habits can become someone else's party menu. BenchPreS examples show legitimate concise or objective expression already present in the Actor request but unapplied; the tax-letter comparison specifically lost bold formatting, while conciseness remained uncredited across its compared variants. In PersistBench the project-template failure promoted a historical preference for Waterfall to the default. All three are failures of controlling what history does to the answer. They do not establish that a single control knob will solve all three.

In the QAMC-v3.2 review, the reviewer challenged dropping BenchPreS from joint development because of limited profile coverage. The distinction is substantive: the formal-writing objective remains in the algorithm design, while the limited contrast and profile coverage restrict what the paper can infer. The decisive next diagnosis for BenchPreS is whether already-visible preferences can be realized without removing required content or introducing misuse. Comparing more selectors alone cannot isolate that gap.

The 40-task Scope change has a real development signal with trade-offs. On RPEval, Match is 44/96 against 38/96 for the unchanged parent, OPB 25 against 29 and UPB 17 against 16. On the ten paired tasks without known flags from either scorer output, Match is 34/69 against 28/69. This preserves the direction after flag filtering, but uses the same exposed fit panel. The net gain still depends on the birthday source; the slice of tasks containing a C obligation drops from 18/33 to 15/33, where 33 is the count of all scored positions in the slice. PersistBench is unchanged for this candidate on the fit panel. [Full 40-task results](ACTOR_MUTATION_READOUT.md)

## Decision after the expansion

- Judge the exact Scope versus parent contrast on native metrics, source distribution, coverage and observed legal use. Compare with Direct and the archived specialist separately. A failure to realize allowed history should lead to a generation-side hypothesis, and damaged C or BU behavior should lead to a narrower exposure or use hypothesis.
- If BenchPreS stays inconclusive because the expansion has only one profile, report its formal-delivery results as limited-scope evidence and retain it as a development objective. A wider score does not solve the missing contextual-flip contrast, even with more tasks from the same profiles.
- If the paper's central claim requires a contextual flip, qualify PairPref free generation as a separate controlled test. Its paper reports 1,227 pairs with the preference and request held fixed while situation changes. Before using it, verify downloadable author rows, grouping by preference and request family, scorer access, hedging/coverage handling, and matched raw-generation and gate controls. Current verification reached the paper and its published baseline results. The data and runnable release remain unverified. PairPref's reported answer quality is situational fit, which does not replace BenchPreS's formal-delivery coverage.
- Remove or replace a primary evaluation only for a demonstrated mismatch with the narrowed paper claim and a qualified replacement, keeping the earlier panels and outcomes visible. A low score provides no estimate of construct mismatch.
