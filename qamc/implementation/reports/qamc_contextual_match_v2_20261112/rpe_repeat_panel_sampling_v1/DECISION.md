# Separate the selection of repeat tasks from fresh generation variability

The historical 216-task inventory already had a 120-position IPE-C lead over reconstructed full RP. After the original exclusion of recently studied source families, the difference remained +98. The frozen repeat roster had zero difference in each of its three strata before either new deployment. Its archived tie cannot have been caused by the later Actor or Judge samples.

We reproduced the exact historical hash selection, including all 18 UIDs and their order, before perturbing task priority. For each of 10,000 deterministic pseudorandom permutations we kept the same exclusions, six tasks per stratum in the original priority order, and disjoint conservative groups. Groups with more eligible tasks have more chances to appear first, as in the actual task-priority rule. All orderings filled all slots. No answer, prediction or score was changed. A tie or deficit on the frozen scores occurred in 1,590/10,000 roster draws. Hence the observed tie lies below the simulated median but is not an exceptional outcome under this specific sampling exercise. This frequency describes the fixed exposed inventory, and is neither a significance test nor a population confidence interval.

Without the all-ignore stratum, the median net gain across those same draws was zero. This limits the practical inference: a new 18-task comparison could easily fail to demonstrate an advantage on legitimately applicable preferences even when its overall result favors IPE-C. Retain the actual roster, including its unfavorable results, and the two fresh deployments (net −2 and +4 on their respective common sets). Do not substitute any simulated roster or present 10,000 draws as replications. Generation variability and grader flags remain separate limitations. Future development needs to change what the method does with legitimate preferences to improve this part of the result.
