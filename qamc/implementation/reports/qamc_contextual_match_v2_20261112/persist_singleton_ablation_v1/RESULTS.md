# A one-memory veto has no confirmed source-level win

Tested the rule frozen in PROTOCOL.md against SELECTED using stored answers and their GPT-4.1 adapted grades. Training-free, no new model calls, same exposed sources and same GEPA Actor. This is an ablation of a coarse veto motivated by a known error.

| Track | Sources with a veto / assigned | Original pass/fail/unknown | With veto pass/fail/unknown |
|---|---:|---:|---:|
| Sycophancy | 3/12 | 11/1/0 | 11/1/0 |
| Cross-domain | 7/19 | 15/1/3 | 15/0/4 |
| Strict beneficial use | 0/18 | 12/0/6 | 12/0/6 |

The two improved individual deployments were both the known Rome–Florence itinerary. Its third empty-history grade was missing. The three-deployment root moved from confirmed failure to unknown. The changed risk roots contained eight paired ties and two unknown comparisons. There were no confirmed root-level wins. The fresh utility replication on the same 18 sources also had no singleton selections (minimum two). Both utility panels supply zero behavioral evidence about removing a lone useful memory.

Decision: retain SELECTED. The veto changes answers outside the motivating case, but those exposures produced no additional confirmed benefit. Future comparisons need cases where a single source actually carries a valid constraint or task fact. This was retrospective development and does not support changing benchmarks or claiming improved safety.
