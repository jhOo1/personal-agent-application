# Benchmark scope decision after development

Recommendation made after observing prior results and before current Gate feedback scores. Use RPEval and PersistBench as primary tests of the same hypothesis: changing which history reaches the generator can prevent irrelevant history from steering decisions while retaining useful evidence. Keep BenchPreS as a reported boundary for expression in third-party deliverables. Preserve the frozen comparison already running. This recommendation changes neither its metrics nor the historical three-benchmark outcomes.

- [RPEval endpoint comparison](../ipe_c_two_endpoint_completion_v1/READOUT.md): 953/1462 with IPE-C, 874 without history, 824 with RP. Only-B is 145/278 vs 142 and 147. [Second backbone](../ipe_c_second_backbone_v1/READOUT.md) repeats the aggregate direction on 22 exposed tasks.
- [PersistBench repeated deployments](../persist_exposure_fr3_v1/FINAL.md): 7/12 sycophancy failure roots for full history, 3/12 for group exposure and 1/12 after selection. Strict beneficial use has 12 known passes and six unknown for selection; the author rubric passes all 18. The cost of indiscriminate suppression is observable.
- [BenchPreS repeated draws](../benchpres_repeat_matched_v1/FINAL_READOUT.md): nine profiles favored Gate on misuse on available comparisons. Non-bold appropriate realization was 95/122 vs 96/122, even though both inputs contained the exact preference on 119 positions. This identifies a downstream realization problem. Missingness, author-judge differences and prior exposure constrain publication claims.

The experiments establish different bottlenecks, not mathematical incompatibility. Selective exposure is a shared hypothesis; the winning selectors and published-method comparisons are not yet a single confirmed system. Prioritize a single frozen exposure policy with the same information rights on the two main tasks; compare with RP-Reasoner and the author PersistBench defenses, including strict beneficial use.

If a third primary benchmark becomes necessary, [MemSyco-Bench](https://github.com/XMUDeepLIT/MemSyco-Bench) provides scope control, memory-evidence conflict and positive personalization within the same suite. Audit fixed-input protocol parity, previous QAMC exposure and competing methods before selecting it. Report benchmark selection as post-development and keep all BenchPreS results visible.
