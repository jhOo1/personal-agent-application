# Feasibility-conditioned answer commitment

This development test checks whether choosing a complete, feasible conditional answer can help where editing one span leaves an invalid recommendation elsewhere. One shared generation produces a direct answer, a conditional answer if justified, and a free preference over the resulting answers and the strong draft. The guarded condition uses the same receipt and substitutes the conditional answer only if the draft location and two distinct visible-source quotes are exact and the model reports a contradicted or unverified precondition. These are structural checks. They do not establish semantic applicability or the quality of the alternate answer. A missing or malformed generation remains missing in both arms. An invalid gate leaves the free choice in place and is counted. No post-score choice.

The eight deliberately exposed sources include two known utility gaps plus RPEval group sizing, furnishings and a coastal itinerary, a group meal protection case, and two formal-delivery protections. All shared inputs and baseline answers are re-linked from the frozen transactional reassembly roster. Permanent memory, scorer and sealed data are untouched. RPEval uses the archived detailed GPT-4.1 protocol, BenchPreS the Qwen adaptation and PersistBench the GPT-4.1 rubric adaptation.

Count requests by unique task and answer. Report original per-benchmark metrics, assigned and common denominators, trigger counts, failures and the observed action. The pilot needs guarded-versus-free actual improvement on two distinct roots, at least one primary score improvement over both free and strong draft, and no observed loss of legal B/C, strict utility, or task completion to earn a larger test. A default or suggested venue must really change. Saying there is a risk, using an optional aside, or scoring identically without different behavior is insufficient. This is a targeted development test.
