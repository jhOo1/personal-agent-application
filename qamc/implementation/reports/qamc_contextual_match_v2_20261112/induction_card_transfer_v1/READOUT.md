# Can the MemAdapter induction-only operation be tested without rebuilding memory?

Yes on 16/18 tasks from the already frozen RPEval development panel. The archived full MemAdapter method first predicts, from the original source memories alone, situations in which a memory could or could not apply. It produces a card per source. Its full system then runs query-conditioned reflection and a guided generator. The authors' separate Stage-1-only generator instead receives the current query, original memories and those initial cards. It can produce a new answer outside the FULL/EMPTY endpoint pool. Neither the full method's final answer nor an intermediate card is a measured answer of this ablation.

Reconstructed the exact Stage-1 request and validated the original reply against the pinned authors' schema and the stored parsed cards. Across the archive there are 300 method rows, 276 verifiable original requests, 265 reusable card sets and 261 of those with successful full-method answers. On the frozen 18-task panel, 17 wires verify and 16 yield valid card sets with 93 cards. The two missing sets remain missing. No new Actor or Judge calls and no held-out data. The aggregate archive is the QAMC formal multi-preference exposed development archive.

In the authors' Table 3, moving from direct generation to induction alone raises personalized memory use accuracy from 49.00 to 81.00 with NaiveRAG and from 58.33 to 78.67 with A-MEM. Three-stage reflection reaches 80.00 and 85.00 respectively. These are the authors' MemSyco results, not ours. This motivates testing whether these generated conditions help on actual RPEval soft-preference decisions, while leaving open whether ordinary extra inference is as effective. A fair control must match both the preparation and final generation budget, count the cached preparation at its cold cost, and retain identical history. Benefits must beat a strong alternative and be observed across distinct cases. If only control and induction both improve over direct generation, the improvement is shared.
