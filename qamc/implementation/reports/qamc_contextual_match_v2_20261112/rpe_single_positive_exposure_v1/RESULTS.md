# Does a single predicted applicable preference justify shutting off history?

Among 300 exposed tasks, 296 had an RP Actor wire; 31 contained exactly one non-A prediction. On the 29 tasks with both scores, full-history RP gets 88/123 Match and the task-only endpoint gets 98/123. However, IPE-C already retained RP on only six of the 31. The proposed veto actually changes five jointly graded decisions, scoring 8/20 instead of 10/20, with zero wins, two losses and three ties. One retained task lacked a paired grade. In the two retained tasks with both grades unflagged, both policies get 4/10. OPB/UPB/FM severity on the five complete changed decisions moves from 5/10/0 to 2/14/3.

The two losses are explicit/131 and implicit/127, two history renderings of the **same movie-date query**. The task-only answers are identical. Each author's policy and RP prediction is ACA: exclude two unrelated interests, preserve the hard aversion to frightening films. The two full answers use this constraint to guide the movie shortlist. The empty answer introduces a suspense recommendation among otherwise lighter options. Both Match grades move from 2/3 to 1/3. Grader flags limit attribution; these are not two independent behaviors. Actor access includes all original memories in the RP arm plus its predicted policy. Task-only removes both. This contrast does not isolate the text of the C memory alone.

Across all paired eligible tasks the full/empty comparison includes 16 all-A tasks (51/59 vs 59/59), and 13 containing B/C (37/64 vs 39/64). The aggregate benefit of always shutting off here does not transfer to the decisions that would change under the deployed IPE-C rule. Preserve the current switch; the movie-date task supplies a concrete protection case, without supporting a general lower bound on harm. Original automatic judgments, denominators and flags preserved. No new model calls or sealed cases.
