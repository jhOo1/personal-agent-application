# Repeated objective-style failure in a BenchPreS appeal

On the exposed denied-claim appeal, all three Strong Gate drafts and all three author Selectivity drafts receive completeness 5 and no credit for the applicable objective-information preference. The preference is present in both exact Actor wires. This task is one of two common persistent non-bold misses after four waves. The other is a brevity preference on a rental-history explanation. The six drafts are repetitions on one task. [Delivery trace](WAVES0123_DELIVERY_TRACE.json)

## What the drafts assert

The task requests a message appealing a denied claim. The fixed facts that reach the Actor describe insurance and a medical condition and some care history. The provided text does not identify the denied claim as being for the documented condition or treatments. All six drafts nonetheless state a specific relationship between the denied claim and the existing care history. They turn related background into purported facts about a particular claim. Some describe the disputed service more specifically than others. Each still delivers a recognizable appeal. A neutral tone, evidence visibility, and a completeness score of 5 can therefore coexist with an unsupported factual relationship.

The archived content-first pilot already included this task. Direct and content-first both have completeness 5. Content-first is credited for objective presentation and direct generation is not. Content-first still states that the denied services were for the recorded condition. The score flip does not close the factual gap. This is an execution-side comparison of actual texts and scoring fields. The preference Judge saw the preference and the answer, without the source facts needed to verify the claim relationship. The completeness Judge saw the task and the answer. It therefore cannot verify that relationship either. [Original pilot](../native_residual_v1/BENCH_REALIZATION_RESULTS.md)

A separate first-wave admissions explanation shows the same type of grounding risk on a different task. Historical qualifications and an academic outcome are available, but the drafts supply specific explanations for the outcome that the input does not establish. One method receives objective-style credit. These two instances do not make source-grounded correctness and style identical. They show why a style or completeness improvement requires a factual protection check. [Admissions trace](NONBOLD_TRACE.md)

## Earlier control and implication

The original Grounded Delivery experiment already compared a specialized assertion/proposal instruction with an equally budgeted generic review, keeping each seed's exact evidence and subsequent Actor request fixed except the system suffix. Its lawyer-documentation case shows that the current dispute's identity can be missing even when several old disputes are present. The specialized Gate draft avoided choosing an unverified old dispute, but deferred the actual documentation and scored 2 for completeness, versus 5 for ordinary review. On seven common BenchPreS cases, the specialized Gate arm and ordinary review both obtain AAR 11/12 and completeness 4.00 and 4.14, respectively. Archived Gate obtains AAR 11/12 and completeness 5.00. The specialized arm was closed. The source repository records that decision at `241c52e67` under `window_07_grounded_delivery_v1/DECISION.md` and verifies the exact replay at `b7079b40d`. A useful request for review can retain factual background and still distinguish it from the unverified reason for the current denial. The existing comparison rules out treating a general cautionary suffix as an untested solution. A future candidate needs a different operation that can preserve the known case facts and retain an actionable appeal when a specific relationship is absent. The prior lawyer case must protect delivery under missing detail. The present appeal provides a distinct source with the same grounding issue. A matched experiment also needs at least one case with a documented relationship that the response should preserve. Compare actual delivery and unsupported assertions alongside unchanged benchmark scoring.

No new policy is frozen or shown to work here. All answers and scores are retained. Zero new model requests, zero independent human reviews, and no access to sealed data.
