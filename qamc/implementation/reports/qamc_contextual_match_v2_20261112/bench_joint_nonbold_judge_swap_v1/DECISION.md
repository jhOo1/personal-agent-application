# The joint-delivery lead survives a change to one grading component

Use the archived three-draw Gate vs Figure 11 Selectivity comparison, separately from the five-method single-panel comparison. Replace only verdicts on appropriate non-bold attributes with the stored masked GPT-4.1 verdict on the identical answer and attribute. Keep Qwen verdicts on inappropriate use, appropriate bold formatting and task completeness. A joint pass requires completeness 5/5 and correct behavior on every attribute. The census covers 144 assigned task/draw pairs. Repeated draws share tasks and profiles.

On the same 101 completely graded pairs, joint pass counts move from 57 vs 28 to 72 vs 33. The Gate advantage therefore rises from 29 to 39 passes. On 75 pairs containing an appropriate non-bold attribute the counts move from 36 vs 16 to 51 vs 21. The remaining 26 pairs provide a negative control: neither arm's count changes. When missing grades are allowed both outcomes, Gate retains at least 14 additional joint passes on the 144 assigned draws under the hybrid rule. A known failing component rules out a joint pass even with an unknown non-bold grade. Incomplete original strict grades are conservatively treated as unknown in the full-allocation bounds.

This supports robustness to replacing one component of the adapted scorer. It is not a second full evaluator, an official DeepSeek-R1 result or a human preference study. The second scorer sees an individual attribute and answer without the original situation, so evaluator identity and presentation change together. Comparing counts with the common five-method panel requires its own denominator. In the separate [attribute-only census](../benchpres_repeat_matched_v1/NONBOLD_CENSUS_READOUT.md), the small non-bold preference ranking itself changes sign; a robust joint delivery lead does not establish non-bold superiority in isolation.
