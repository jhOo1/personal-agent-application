# Does the superset requirement add anything beyond candidate count?

On the original 49-task replay a count-only rule makes the same eight changes as strict containment, including both previously identified BU improvements. This control checks that both choices are nonempty and the reserve is longer. It never inspects source identity. Because every selected endpoint is identical, the two rules also have identical answers, grades and missingness. The original JSON was reproduced byte for byte after adding this control. Run bash run.sh --count-control for the comparison.

For the third archived risk deployment the design was committed in the original repository at 48f3d2fbb before joining its scores. On the 12 SYC roots neither rule changes a single answer, under either reserve choice. On the 19 CD roots both rules change one answer with deployment 2 as reserve or three answers with deployment 1. GPT paired excess failures relative to keeping deployment 3 are zero in both cases. Qwen bounds are [-1,0] in each comparison because a missing primary grade can become a scored pass. Keep unknown scores and do not interpret the two reserve comparisons as independent samples. No CD or SYC task distinguishes the two rules; the SYC outcome provides no test of safety under additional exposure. Both runs reuse complete saved endpoints and use the original adapted grading protocols. None of this establishes noninferiority or a selective advantage on the benchmark's official scorer.

Decision: keep the two BU behavior repairs in the development record. Drop the specific claim that testing subset membership caused them. Additional collection for this mechanism would only be informative if sources actually differ at the same or competing cardinalities and if the resulting action can change. The two previously observed RPEval count-matched A-for-B exchanges did not improve Match. No new benchmark requests or sealed data. Reproduce the risk analysis with bash third_draw.sh.
