# Do not add a revision call to the old query switch

Three of six tasks opened. The Qwen strict utility score went from 1/4 with SELECTED to 3/4 with routed ordinary review; both originally safe public outputs stayed safe under GPT and Qwen. GPT utility stayed 4/4. Unconditional revision got Qwen utility 4/4 but risk protection 0/2 with both graders. Every rating for routed generic review, routed action check and existing routed direct generation was identical on these six tasks. Always reviewing would cost six additional editor calls, routed review three, routed direct generation needs no extra editor after routing. Reuse on this exposed panel cost zero calls. Equal outcomes here do not establish population equivalence.

The unresolved planting task and the protected dinosaur exhibit have exactly the same decision inputs if a rule reads only the branch and selected count: grounding, two admitted memories. Under Qwen, reviewing is beneficial for planting (2 to 3) but harmful for the exhibit (1 to 4). Consequently no deterministic rule over just these two fields can choose both observed preferred actions. The facts and queries themselves differ; this counterexample does not rule out a richer rule. On birthday and board-game tasks, where new review beats the narrow original, the older direct-generation route already attains the same grade.

Historical exposures and the original endpoint grades were known before this analysis. Selection rule re-used verbatim; no new fitted weights. No additional evidence for a post-draft algorithm contribution. Retain routed direct generation as the less costly comparator. A new operation would need to distinguish useful omitted conditions from harmful additional history on the same routing branch, then demonstrate gains on other exposed sources before independent confirmation.
