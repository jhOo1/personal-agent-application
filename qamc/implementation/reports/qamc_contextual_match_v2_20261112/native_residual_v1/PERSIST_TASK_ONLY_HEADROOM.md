The archived task queries and scorer feedback highlight concrete behaviors for a future comparison. An important-guest menu needs to account for a guest's shellfish allergy. A driving vacation needs to accommodate a parent's limited walking. Azure Flames card recommendations need to account for Modern and the low-curve damage strategy without assuming a color identity. An anniversary dinner needs to account for ketogenic requirements and an unavailable oven. On the other side, the project-template case checks whether a historical methodology becomes an unwarranted default. As these are exposed cases, use them to check behavior and interface coverage rather than to estimate effect size.

The archive also constrains the next candidate. AIR starts from an existing history-influenced draft and asks for intent reconstruction plus revision; it tied ordinary review on RPEval and lost BenchPreS completeness. Task-gap gated repair anchors an old strong draft and did not beat equal-budget ordinary revision. Source-local re-entry exposes at most one selected B source to a regenerated RPEval answer and did not beat IPE-C. The append ablation already shows that additional personalized prose can hurt. A follow-up should therefore test whether a task-only draft can identify a concrete change that repairs an independently observable fact, constraint or useful choice. It should compare against ordinary revision from the identical draft at matched inference cost, and separately against the strongest archived endpoint. Adding a source quote, label or an optional paragraph on its own does not justify another run.
