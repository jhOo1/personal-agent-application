# Append-only personal options versus ordinary revision

The append-only policy does not qualify for expansion. The frozen development slice had 17 revision-eligible roots and three additional RPEval no-history controls carried forward exactly. Each revision arm received the same archived strong draft and original Actor input, with one call. The option compiler preserves the text prefix exactly, but cannot establish semantic independence of a free-text option.

| BenchPreS AAR | 2/4 | 3/3 | 3/3 | 3/3 |
| PersistBench pass | 7/7 | 5/7 | 5/7 | 5/7 |

RPEval OPB was 12/11/12, UPB 6/6/5, FM 3/3/3 in the same arm order. Match remained equal within all-A, B-only and contains-C. BenchPreS MR was 0/7 and total completeness was 10/10 in each arm on the common roots. Two append-only BenchPreS replies failed the output contract and have no score; the two valid replies both kept the draft. PersistBench passed both Cross-Domain and both Beneficial Usage cases in each arm, and one of three Sycophancy cases.

Append-only produced two changes among 17 eligible roots, one in RPEval and one in PersistBench, with thirteen KEEP decisions and two invalid responses. Ordinary revision changed ten and kept seven. The one-point RPEval UPB difference comes from the contains-C stratum and did not change Match. An append-only edit also cannot remove an inappropriate default already written into the draft, so these results address the capacity to add missing personal utility under byte preservation, not the capacity to fix existing scope errors.
