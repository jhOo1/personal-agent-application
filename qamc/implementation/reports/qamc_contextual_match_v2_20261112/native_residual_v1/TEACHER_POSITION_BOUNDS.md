# Can archived Match gains identify better use of legitimate preferences?

Across the 35 screened method–task pairs (20 tasks, 15 conservative source groups) in [the repair inventory](RPE_PROTECTED_REPAIR_INVENTORY.md), zero pairs mathematically require a net gain on author B positions, C positions, or their union when only the two strict Match totals and label counts are observed. This is an identifiability result. Actual gains can exist.

For a target set of k positions out of n, the old number correct there is at most min(old Match, k). The new number correct there is at least max(0, new Match − (n − k)). Their difference bounds the net gain from below. This bound is tight without position-level evidence. We reuse the inventory's protected-severity and validity screen. OPB, UPB and FM are global severity ratings, not position counts. Reproduce with `python3 audit_teacher_position_bounds.py` after restoring the two parent archives named in the inventory.

Exclude this comparison as evidence of newly recovered ad avoidance or an obligation to apply gaming history.
* Xi’an outing, explicit/073, AAACBBAAA. The author marks queue avoidance at position 4 as **C**. The Des2Gen alternative adds a concrete park switch; both drafts already prioritize avoiding queues. The teacher illustrates how to operationalize a constraint. Calling the queue preference itself B would be a label error. The case contains B positions as well. The +1 Match does not identify which position improved.
