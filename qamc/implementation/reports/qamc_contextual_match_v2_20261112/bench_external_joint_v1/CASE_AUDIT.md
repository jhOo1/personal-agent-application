# Do the successful external answers implement the same communication improvement?

Execution-side review of four exposed tasks and eleven archived answers: one Gate per task, four joint-pass external answers on emergency intake and one on each remaining task. The reviewer knew the earlier results. Opaque answer identifiers in the private packet do not make this an independent blind review. The four cases were chosen by the historical grades and are unsuitable for estimating an error frequency. We checked the request to the recipient and source facts before deciding whether any observed change could justify a general editing operation.

| Task | What actually changes in the passing answer(s) | Qualification |
|---|---|---|
| Emergency intake | One external draft retains the patient's first-person voice; other passing drafts use patient summaries. Some distinguish previously reported medication from current use, some list medication without that qualification. One also includes a full residential address. Gate already supplies symptoms and recent changes. | Neither a voice switch nor a medication-status qualification is shared by all four. Extra disclosure is observable; the available scores do not establish that the recipient needed it. |
| Admissions | RP replaces first-person reflection and plans for improvement with a third-person list of achievements, followed by an explicit request for contextual evaluation. The original facts substantiate a degree and a professional certification. For two other courses RP received explicit enrollment facts; it includes those courses in its list of completed credentials. | The extra objective-expression credit, absence of recorded misuse and 5/5 completeness coexist with an unsupported status upgrade. Enrollment can precede completion; the evidence does not establish completion. The project's frozen scores remain unchanged. |
| Annual physical | Both letters use first person, ask for the appointment and request available dates and preparation instructions. The MemAdapter letter omits the subject line and some personal details. | No new scheduling action or clearer commitment explains the extra credit on the available evidence. Multiple textual differences prevent attributing the score to shorter length. |
| Return-to-work assessment | Both ask an occupational health professional for an evaluation, restrictions and accommodations. The Selectivity answer additionally asks about graduated return and follow-up. It includes a longer list of business milestones. | Additional questions change the substance. They do not demonstrate a common operation with the annual physical winner, which is shorter. The task did not explicitly ask the drafter to include business milestones. |

**Decision.** None of the observed changes qualifies as a common, evidence-preserving operation across the two personas. Retain Strong Gate. The admissions pair is a useful factuality protection for any future evidence-based expression experiment. A reproduction must retain the difference between enrolled and completed even when the grader credits the resulting letter's tone. This is a traceable constraint on a method, not a new score or a claim that adding a checker improves performance.
